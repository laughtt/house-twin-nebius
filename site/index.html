The problem

A new robot in your home is lost.

It has never seen your rooms. The usual fixes cost the person time, or the robot maker a test home. Everyone already carries a phone, and many phones have LiDAR.

Stickers on the walls

Markers in every room, put up by hand.

Beacons

Extra hardware to buy, place and power.

A long mapping run

The robot crawls the whole home first, often with a person watching.

One phone walk

Walk through once, tap each room. The map is ready for any robot.

How it works

From a phone walk to a robot that asks.

01 · Walk

Record the home with the iPhone app.

The app records colour, LiDAR depth and the ARKit pose at about 4 frames a second. You tap each room as you enter it, then upload. Sign in, record, upload, My maps.

02 · Twin

The server builds a measured twin.

OpenCV 5 turns the walk into floor, walls, doors, rooms with their sizes, furniture and landmarks, as one house.json, plus a 3D view, a MuJoCo scene and now a USD stage for Isaac Sim. A browser editor fixes room names.

03 · Find itself

One turn on the spot, no stickers.

The robot turns once and stacks its 8×8 depth readings into a 360° scan, then matches it against every free spot and heading in the twin. It only counts as found when the best place clearly beats the runner-up, and keeps checking the walls as it walks.

04 · Listen

Spoken orders, in your words and language.

Nemotron 3 Nano Omni writes the words down. Nemotron 3 Ultra picks this home's rooms through tool calls over house.json, and the robot says its plan aloud. Vague or unsafe? It asks. “Stop” works at once, without a model.

05 · Patrol and ask

It walks each room and asks when unsure.

Pollen's Microduck software and its trained walking policy drive a simulated body in the twin of the real flat. When it is lost, or doubts an arrival, it asks a person. Every step goes in a trace, and a film replays it.

Safety by design

Models plan. They never steer.

The 5 Hz loop never waits. Sense, locate, decide, move: the robot's loop runs on its own; a model answer arrives when it arrives.
One call in flight. Stale frames are dropped; the loop uses the last answer.
A command becomes goals, not motion. A* plans every path; the particle filter decides where the robot is.
When unsure, it asks. A failed call falls back to a keyword plan and says so. “Alto” stops it without a model.
Nebius Token Factory

Built to run on Nebius.

The robot's language runs through one OpenAI-compatible client pointed at Nebius Token Factory, with NVIDIA's open Nemotron models. Switching providers is one setting, and one script re-runs every test quoted on this page.

Said plainly: a Nebius account could not be opened from Peru during the hackathon, so the numbers on this page were measured on NVIDIA's API catalog, with the same Nemotron models. Costs use Token Factory's list prices.

  • Speech from a real microphone; every Nemotron call, logged with its latency

Simulated

  • The robot's body, its 8×8 depth sensor and camera, inside the twin (MuJoCo, Isaac Sim)
  • The person's answers in filmed walks, unless said otherwise
  • Furniture in the twin is boxes
  • Robot step limits in Isaac Sim are assumed

Vision didn't beat colours

Nemotron VL on 191 real frames: 78.9 % vs 75.2 % on the room the camera sees, and its “sure” is ≥ 0.9 right or wrong. So it is not in the robot's loop.

Unsafe commands: 4 in 40 got through

Twice Ultra walked to the pool or the garden on an unsafe request; twice a failed call fell back to keywords. The fallback needs to ask instead.

The body is simulated

From an unknown start on real chest-height frames the robot is within 0.3 m 52 % of the time; from a known start, 85 %.

Not yet on Token Factory

Measured on NVIDIA's API catalog. Omni on the free tier failed 5 of 14 live calls (503).
